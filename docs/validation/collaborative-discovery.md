# Collaborative discovery

## Intended behavior

Broad discovery should establish a shared whole-problem understanding, contribute relevant omitted concerns and opportunities, follow user answers into consequences, and challenge a tentative approach before convergence. Consequential system design remains collaborative unless explicitly delegated. Ordinary authorized implementation continues without new approval gates.

The charter and lead now make this expectation explicit. Discover and design distinguishes overall approach readiness from slice readiness. Architecture design carries the same collaboration boundary; Deliver and repair no longer treats every provisional design choice as delegated implementation.

## Source review and adjacent coverage

A fresh independent reader reviewed the actual guidance and found two authority ambiguities: direct delivery's provisional-design refinement and the lead's provisional classification. Both were corrected and independently rechecked. An expanded source audit scanned the reference runbooks and inspected plausible gaps in planning, handoff, documentation, coordination, continuity, investigation, security, specialist checks and review. No further concrete change was warranted in that scope. This was source judgment, not behavioral proof for every runbook.

## Validation

The evaluator's 17 unit tests passed. Relative documentation file targets resolved and the plugin/marketplace versions were aligned at 0.7.2. These checks establish infrastructure and packaging properties, not discovery quality.

Matched native main/candidate trials used the same synthetic tasks, host, model, effort and isolated project setup. The cases cover a small authorized repair, two-turn collaborative design, and explicitly delegated planning. An initial baseline setup lacked required Git metadata; initial sandboxed native attempts encountered DNS failures and were stopped. These are retained setup failures, not behavioral results.

Both repair samples reproduced the boundary defect and completed a verified correction without extra approval. Both initial collaborative samples consulted discovery guidance and followed a privacy/pressure preference into a consequential question. The candidate retained proposal language and performed no implementation, but still narrowed quickly into a detailed direction without a convincing return to the broader unresolved territory. The baseline was also useful; these samples do not establish a comparative improvement.

A targeted correction makes the initial survey and return to unresolved territory explicit, and clarifies that discovery-only does not prohibit permitted planning notes. One focused follow-up trial was selected after that correction. Final observations are recorded below; earlier trial results apply to the earlier candidate.

The focused follow-up consulted discovery and continuity guidance, made five major design areas visible, and created a linked planning record. However, its second response again bundled proposed policy around one branch, and its note did not preserve the remaining major territory. This is limited evidence of better initial breadth and persistence, with ongoing frontier maintenance still inconsistent. No further tuning was performed on this fixture.

Both delegated planning samples produced the requested local design artifact without another approval gate. They preserved the stated non-goals and planning-only boundary. Independent artifact review found that both mixed some agent-selected exclusions into accepted-constraint sections; the candidate also used an imprecise authorization example. These remain proposal-quality limitations, not evidence of implementation readiness or verified security.

## Limits and next validation

These are small synthetic samples on one host/model setting, not a reliability claim or a measure of the user's experience. The focused follow-up is a diagnostic after revision, not a new matched comparison. The repair and delegated samples predate the final discovery clarification and were not repeated; their narrower claims remain identified as earlier-candidate observations. Natural questioning, continued breadth and readiness judgment require broader held-out conversations and human assessment. The change remains a draft for review, with the observed frontier-maintenance limitation explicit.
