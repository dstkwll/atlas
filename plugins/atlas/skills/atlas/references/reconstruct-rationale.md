# Reconstruct design rationale

Use when asked why a design exists, or when changing an unusual constraint requires understanding its origin. Start with current behavior and accepted decisions; use [Understand existing behavior](understand-behavior.md) when the mechanism itself is unclear. This investigation produces evidence and recommendations. It does not authorize removing a guard or restoring a historical design.

Anchor the question to the relevant symbol, behavior or configuration and source revision. Follow its introduction and meaningful changes, including renames, linked reviews, decisions and incidents when available. Search for the specific unresolved reason rather than surveying every connected system. Read enough of the original discussion to distinguish a proposal, a rejected alternative and the decision actually made. A commit message or later summary can point to evidence without fully explaining it.

Separate the explanation into what an inspected source explicitly records, what you infer from surrounding evidence, and what remains unknown. Check whether an explanation fits the chronology and competing evidence. A performance benefit visible today does not establish that performance motivated the original choice. Current code, historical intent and current accepted requirements may disagree; preserve that disagreement instead of inventing a coherent story.

Interpret operational history in its measurement context. Retention, sampling, changed instrumentation, issue regrouping and inaccessible discussions can hide events. An empty search is narrower than evidence of absence. A nearby release and falling error count suggest a hypothesis; they do not by themselves establish a fix. Include the relevant time window, source status and access limits when they affect the conclusion, while keeping protected records in their approved environment.

Test whether the historical constraint still applies using current contracts, dependencies and available checks. An old workaround may still protect an external consumer; a once-valid restriction may have been superseded. Neither age nor apparent elegance settles that question. If resolving it requires unavailable evidence or new product judgment, identify the precise gap and the safe work that can continue.

Return the answer at the depth the reader needs, with the sources that support it, material alternatives or contradictions, and implications for the proposed change. Preserve uncertainty in diagrams and simplified explanations. Record durable accepted decisions through [Continuity and decisions](continuity-and-decisions.md); discovered rationale alone is not newly accepted policy. Stop when the question is sufficiently answered or a specific evidence boundary prevents a stronger conclusion. No history dossier or new document is required for a short answer.
