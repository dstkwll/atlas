# Show progress toward the outcome

Use when the user needs the route to done, current work, dependencies or available parallel work. A flowchart fits dependencies; lanes or a compact board may fit mostly independent work. Choose from the actual structure, not a preference for a diagram style.

Represent high-level outcomes with enough detail to expose the next useful action. Base statuses on conversation and inspected evidence. Distinguish completed, actively underway, ready, blocked and not yet started where useful; proposed work remains proposed. Unknown status is unknown. A plan to start is not evidence of activity, and an agent's completion claim may still need verification. Keep verification pending visible when it matters to calling an outcome done.

Use dependency edges only for real prerequisites. Show what can proceed in parallel separately from what is actually running. Name an owner only when known. Surface a blocker with its unmet prerequisite or needed decision, rather than a decorative warning. Do not invent dates, percentages or assignments to fill a layout.

For example, accessibility research and volunteer planning may proceed independently, while booking depends on the accepted venue and applicable authorization. Completing the research does not complete or authorize the booking. A failed check should change the affected task's status and next step, not erase other verified progress.

Check that the user can distinguish what is done, what is being worked on, what is available next and what cannot yet proceed. Keep the accepted outcome visible enough to reveal a route that has drifted from it.
