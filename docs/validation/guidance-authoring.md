# Guidance authoring: evidence and limitations

2026-09-26. This is a public, behavior-focused account of the connected-core writing pilot and the subsequent five-wave authoring program. It separates **source preservation** from **native behavior**. The reusable cases and review expectations remain in [the evaluator](../../evals/README.md), including the [connected-core rubric](../../evals/core-authoring-review.md) and [interrupted-integration rubric](../../evals/interrupted-integration-review.md). No result here establishes general improvement.

## Scope and source identity

The **pilot** compared merged-main `14672e9` (Atlas 0.10.0) with selected source `ed80e27` (0.10.1): two three-turn synthetic cases, calendar design and paused correction/recovery, on native Codex and Copilot against both sources. All eight attempts completed. It is a separate population from the waves.

The **five-wave program** used original `9dcf142` (0.10.1) and successively tested `ac0552b` (0.10.2), `cdd58f5` (0.10.3), `70b0d25` (0.10.4), `cf4b959e` (0.10.5) and `13661349` (0.10.6). The paired baselines were Wave 1 `9dcf142`, Wave 2 `4c976595`, Wave 3 `046d864a` and Wave 4 `70b0d256`. Wave 5 compared cumulative candidate `13661349` with program original `9dcf142`. The wave schedule comprised 52 fixed synthetic attempts across Codex and Copilot: 8, 8, 8, 12 and 16. **51 completed; the Wave 2 candidate Copilot ticket/handoff attempt timed out without a conversational return.** These are attempt and completion counts, not pass rates. Trials explicitly selected Atlas, used Astra at medium effort and a 360-second attempt bound; authoring and source-review model work are different populations. Cases reused development or regression boundaries, not a held-out test of natural requests.

## Source preservation

The pilot retained the shared lead and Wayfinding bodies, reorganized discovery decisions and clarified continuity's existing-artifact, destination and recovery relationships. An initial author's source coverage was incomplete; a separate final source review found no actionable preservation defect in the connected group. That review did not prove equivalent behavior.

Across Waves 1–5, the selected active surface contains **20 changed and 39 byte-retained targets out of 59** relative to the program original. Changes made existing worker-return, failure and general-return guidance easier to locate; connected existing records, conditional fallback, ticket readiness and handoff; clarified same-task caller knowledge, retry links and cross-framework checks; exposed evidence-qualified chart and post-training returns; and clarified existing Wayfinding navigation, domain language, retained exploratory slices and selective-reader heading/link scope. Retained files still carry governing obligations. This is a source disposition, not 20 demonstrated behavioral gains or a new product architecture. Wave 1 retained non-blocking reading risks: cumulative-effort rules could appear limited to self-selected bounds, the general failure-handling return sat beneath a demonstration heading, and three recovery examples could be misread as a closed list. Shared lead rules mitigated those risks; no resulting behavior failure was established.

The first final-wave source assessment did **not** cover the forty earlier-wave originals and was insufficient as a cumulative audit. A fresh original-first assessment examined all 59 original targets, directly read all 20 changed candidate bodies and reused 39 verified byte-identical originals; it found no supported preservation blocker. Earlier reviews also had narrower connected-source or history coverage. The final source was accepted as eligible, not validated for host loading, every historical connection or runtime adherence.

## Collaborative design, recovery and delivery

In the pilot calendar attempts, both sources in both hosts used the export, paper diary and missing holds to challenge a live-availability promise. They compared plausible directions, incorporated changed goals and partial acceptance, left visibility policy open and worked in the existing HTML planning home. In the later four Wave 5 calendar attempts, both sources again challenged that promise, incorporated volunteer/caretaker, no-reservation and timestamp decisions, and kept unconfirmed visibility open rather than implementing or creating another map. **Both baselines were useful.** The Wave 5 Codex attempts omitted applicable user-research and product-interface method consultation before dependent work; a candidate Copilot discovery read remained incomplete without an established downstream design failure. A candidate Codex first-write notice explained the recovery purpose less clearly. Pilot revised-source Copilot temporarily saved an over-narrow claim that the method requires one question at a time; the later record replaced it, and this script did not exercise its consequence. Adaptive pacing was not superseded.

In the pilot and Wave 5 archive/recovery cases, all compared arms suspended previously authorized delivery for discovery, preserved exact paper-label reasoning, obeyed a short checkpoint and used existing files in a **distinct fresh native session** to complete only the explicitly resumed exact-empty-title correction. Native regression checks failed before and passed after the narrow fix; nonempty titles and retention behavior were left alone. The wider design stayed open. Wave 5 candidate Codex has an unresolved fresh-session lead-read gap despite useful completion. Original Codex briefly overstated what naming policy the owner had settled, without implementing extra policy. Both Wave 5 Copilot conversational replies omitted the standing `Next` paragraph while identifying completion and open questions. These are artifact-based fresh-session results, **not compaction or note-alone recovery proof**.

Wave 1's four deferred-hardware attempts all repaired the offline classifier and checked meaningful local regressions under their respective command permissions; both baselines succeeded. No station, acquisition driver or physical repeat-run acceptance was tested. Its four interrupted-return attempts each repaired the local `owner`/null-owner integration within one revision and one test run, preserved request evidence and answered that green local checks did **not** complete the whole task: independent review and the terminal operation outcome remained outstanding. No remote effect was observed or replay requested. Two Copilot arms also left evidence-file-dependent hash instrumentation in a behavior test. Neither scenario established a candidate gain or exhausted-bound behavior. A focused review supplied the behavior judgments after a broader review returned no assessment.

## Planning artifacts and technical composition

Wave 2's four HTML-PRD attempts preserved owner self-release to the unclaimed queue while leaving manager reassignment undecided. Separate readers supplied only the final HTML generally recovered that acceptance, open question and pause; one reader invented an unsupported manager-first ordering, and another reader's event history has a gap. This tests supplied-document recovery, not rendering or compaction. In the four ticket/handoff attempts, useful slices still had consequential qualifications: candidate Codex omitted a return-status contract and return-before-follow-up integration witness; baseline Codex overstated member-directory non-exposure and introduced an invitation-audit gate without a matching decision route. Candidate Copilot made an unjustified post-return lifecycle decision a blanket prerequisite for ordinary persisted return, then **timed out after writing artifacts without a completed return**. Written files cannot establish its conversational authority or continuation after the deadline. These are draft defects or open decisions, not observed operational breaches.

Wave 3's eight quote-architecture and two-turn search-composition attempts produced useful advice in both sources. Quote notes inspected the implementation and callers, kept preparation separate from approval and rejected silent repricing; they did not implement or test quote runtime behavior. **All four quote attempts gave incomplete first-write recovery/location-choice notices**, including an arm that had read continuity. Search advice withdrew mechanisms that could expose details after a workshop or permission change, distinguishing client response guards from server authorization; it neither measured latency nor implemented security. Candidate Copilot mislabeled README/API evidence and also gave an incomplete first-write notice. Baseline success and these misses preclude a source-caused improvement claim.

## Specialist advice, comparisons and precise output

Wave 4's twelve attempts all completed, with useful baseline as well as candidate advice. In the activity transition, all questioned `Reserve now` against manual confirmation, deduplicated repeated feedback and did not turn a reservation request into accepted policy. One candidate Copilot first answer missed the fixed-grid concern and a phone/reflow check. In the chart case, all separated Harbor's 40/400 recorded failures from Hill's 10/20 and 30 unknown outcomes; conditional 20–80% Hill bounds assume the unknown sessions are additional comparable attempts, **not** a confidence interval. They recommended investigation rather than an authorized staffing change. The directly labeled SVG retained a concern about red/green evaluative framing. Both Copilot answers supplied next-action substance without a separate `Next:` heading. In post-training, all found that truncation made two twelve-token chosen/rejected pairs identical while a third retained one differing token, and advised effective-pair and preprocessing checks instead of a longer run. None established corpus prevalence, held-out improvement or actual training; none explicitly proposed checking train/development overlap or development-set reuse for tuning. Baselines already supplied the central distinctions.

Wave 5's four Arena comparisons all declined to name a qualifying winner: Lumen required hosted processing, Feather discarded invalid rows, and Halo lacked established rejected-row lifecycle and all-invalid accounting. That is a recommendation about supplied alternatives, not live tournament or CSV-runtime evidence. All four omitted the first-write recovery/location-choice notice; none consulted continuity. Both Copilot conversational closes lacked explicit forward orientation, although their saved records supplied closure and later-repair conditions. In four exact-output controls every arm returned only `The reports are ready.` without a file mutation; Copilot also invoked the supplied skill redundantly. The lead bytes were unchanged and no changed reference was used, so this control cannot attribute its result to the authoring edits.

## Interpretation and public evidence

These small, explicitly activated synthetic comparisons do **not** establish candidate-caused improvement, implicit routing, natural long-session reliability, context compaction, host/profile loading, rendered or participant outcomes, physical/remote acceptance, or general runtime correctness. Consulted guidance, a completed host turn, an artifact, a passing evaluator test and useful judgment are different claims. First-write notices, consultation gaps, unsupported artifact claims and the Wave 2 timeout remain material despite successful baselines. Source eligibility does not erase them.

The full original reports, including their historical status and qualifications, are preserved in their PR records: [connected-core pilot](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/41#issuecomment-5847279448), [delivery and assurance](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/42#issuecomment-5847279721), [planning artifacts](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/43#issuecomment-5847279926), [technical methods](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/44#issuecomment-5847280171), [specialist methods](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/45#issuecomment-5847280432), [cumulative authoring](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/pull/46#issuecomment-5847280667). This summary and the public cases and rubrics provide the findings and limits needed for public maintenance; detailed traces remain maintainer evidence.

## October 18, 2026: preserving meaning in instruction rewrites

[Issue #52](https://github.com/dstkwll/atlas/issues/52) follows the independent Toolbox draft. The Atlas amendment clarifies unresolved interpretations, the scope of qualifiers and compact terms that can lose obligations or imply extra authority. It retains the existing lead, ordinary editing freedom and explicitly delegated judgment. It introduces no shared plugin dependency, routing change or new handoff skill.

Four paired, fictional drafting cases compared main `57225e63` (Atlas 0.11.0) with the 0.11.1 candidate writer, Git blob `beaf438`. All eight fresh, context-only Copilot **Claude Opus 5.5 / xhigh** calls completed within their 600-second limits. Each arm received the same full Atlas lead, raw task and artifact text; only the writing runbook differed. Referenced files and tools were unavailable. These are text-drafting observations, not native file operations or selection evidence.

| Case | Observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Reused library-instruction diagnostic | The source said to retain requested support privately and “do not infer a diagnosis or add it to public copy.” The baseline embedded a wider ban on public support details and recording diagnoses, then asked for confirmation. The candidate retained the source clause and placed the stronger proposal outside the operative instructions. It still added avoidable confirmation and several policy questions after completing the draft. |
| New rail-instruction compression | Both retained mandatory fees versus optional upgrades, the missing-timestamp route, the specific shorter-connection approval, overnight date, conditional preference and scoped schedule uncertainty. |
| Typo-only control | Both returned exactly the corrected sentence, without a broader rewrite or approval step. |
| Explicitly delegated policy interpretation | Both chose to require a one-sentence business purpose and explained its tradeoff while retaining receipt, project-code and payment-approval requirements. Neither returned the required-versus-optional choice to the owner. |

An independent Opus 5.5/xhigh grader received the tasks, rubrics and shuffled outputs without source identities. It rejected the baseline library rewrite for the expanded privacy rules, accepted the candidate with the question burden noted above, and accepted both versions on the other three tasks. It also rejected two deliberately incorrect controls: an embedded stronger privacy rule and a refusal to make the delegated choice. An intended valid optional-purpose control omitted the explicit evidence-complete drafting gate and was graded partial; that fixture defect was retained, not counted as successful positive calibration.

The library case was reused after observing the related Toolbox failure; its added interpretation rubric is diagnostic, not held out. The other raw cases were frozen before examining outputs. One sample per arm, shared model conditions, synthetic inputs and successful baselines preclude a general improvement or reliability claim. Source review by a separate Copilot Opus 5.5/xhigh context found no product-intent change; its evidence-wording findings prompted narrower historical claims. The unchanged evaluator's 39 tests passed, supporting its existing checks rather than these prose behaviors.

Three separate native Copilot CLI **1.0.91** sessions on macOS arm64 used Opus 5.5/xhigh, temporary configuration/cache/workspaces and read-only tools. With both packages available, a workshop-instruction rewrite invoked Toolbox's writing skill. Selecting the Atlas profile for that same prompt also loaded the Atlas lead and read its writing runbook. An ordinary invitation rewrite invoked no skill or tool. All three completed within 180 seconds; the drafts retained the supplied consent, approval and continuation rules, all 77 fixture files stayed unchanged and the workspaces remained empty.

Those native probes used Toolbox commit `8e4bea37` and an earlier Atlas 0.11.1 writer snapshot (`d13da38`), before “Otherwise, retain” became “For interpretations outside that authority, retain.” They show composition for these prompts, not final-wording adherence, broad routing reliability or a collision requiring correction. Two sandboxed authentication preflights failed; normal OS access used existing authentication without copying credentials. No user installation was changed. The separate [marketplace observations](copilot-plugin.md#local-marketplace-follow-up-october-18-2026) concern registration/discovery and must not be counted as these model trials.
