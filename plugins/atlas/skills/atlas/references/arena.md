# Compare independent alternatives (Arena)

Use when a consequential question has several credible approaches or the first proposal may be anchoring the design. This is an optional runbook for the lead, not a standalone skill, required design stage or fixed model panel. Ordinary comparison can remain within Discover and design without launching an Arena.

Before launching independent candidates, explain the decision to resolve, why diverse attempts could help, the candidate scope, expected artifact and bounded effort. Obtain the user's confirmation of that run unless their explicit request already specifies or accepts it. General permission to design, explore or keep working does not confirm an Arena. Do not silently replace missing confirmation with a self-imposed budget.

Set a concrete total worker/attempt limit and practical deadline before starting. Use cost limits only when spending is observable and enforcement is available; otherwise disclose that limitation and use observable work/time bounds. Respect stricter host/project limits, reserve effort for comparison and verification, and do not restart or add candidates just because the first results disappoint. A changed budget requires renewed agreement. If the host cannot supervise the proposed run within its bounds, narrow it with the user or offer a clearly labelled comparison by the lead.

Give each candidate the same decision context, accepted constraints, permitted capabilities and expected evidence, with an independent writable location when writing is authorized. Use the accepted brief and explicit user amendments as the common reference; distinguish requirements from preferences, provisional choices and unresolved judgment. Let candidates form ideas before showing them one another's answers. Independence does not require a named model family, cloud execution or a communication board. Use available approved host delegation; do not install coordination infrastructure. Missing independent contexts must be reported, not simulated by changing personas.

Choose task-specific comparison criteria before viewing results. First assess whether each proposal meets the accepted requirements, with evidence appropriate to the assignment. A design comparison does not require implementation proof, but unsupported claims remain unverified. Rank eligible proposals on preferences and tradeoffs, including a simpler option when credible; elegance, novelty or an aggregate score cannot compensate for a broken requirement. Read all returned artifacts; record dropouts and incomplete coverage. If none is established as meeting the accepted requirements at the assignment's level of evidence, say that no proposal qualifies. You may recommend a provisional repair direction with its gaps, but do not crown it a final winner by deferring required coverage to later. Keep this status consistent in the saved artifact and closing recommendation; panel pressure to finish does not change eligibility or authorize another round. Agreement is not proof and disagreement does not automatically mean the brief was wrong.

If the agreed run includes peer critique or successive rounds, supply the accepted brief and its authorized amendments to candidates and judges at every round; a previous winner, score or summary must not replace that source. Reassess each revision against it, including proposals that previously qualified. When borrowing an idea, identify what improves and any changed assumptions, requirement coverage or tradeoffs in the existing design reasoning. Peer learning may improve a solution, but the resulting agreement is no longer independent corroboration. Winning more rounds supplies no additional proof of meeting the brief. All rounds, repairs and verification remain within the agreed total bounds.

Keep freedom to improve provisional choices and propose challenges to accepted constraints. A proposed change to the brief remains a separate user decision; do not rank a proposal as meeting the current brief by assuming that change is accepted. When a user amendment is accepted, apply it explicitly to the comparison and recheck affected conclusions.

Synthesize only compatible ideas, then verify the resulting proposal or artifact against the accepted brief rather than relying on evidence for its parents. A candidate can win outright; do not combine weaker pieces merely to use every output. Use [Independent review](independent-review.md) when the decision warrants it; the fresh reviewer derives the requirements from the original assignment and accepted amendments before reading the tournament's winner rationale. Return alternatives, important tradeoffs, recommendation, evidence and uncertainty, plus actual completed versus missing work. Record material comparison reasoning in the existing design record. Recommendations do not accept product choices or authorize implementation/publication beyond the assignment.
