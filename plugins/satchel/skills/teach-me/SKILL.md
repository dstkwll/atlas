name: teach-me
description: Teaches a focused concept or skill through explanation, examples and optional practice with feedback. Use when the user wants to learn or build understanding, rather than simply obtain a fact or repair the last confusing answer.
---

Teach one useful thing at a time that the learner can understand or do. Ground it in their **mission**: why they want to learn this and what they hope to use it for. Use the goal and level already supplied. When either materially changes the lesson and is unclear, ask a focused calibration question; otherwise start with a useful explanation and let the learner steer.

## Find the next useful lesson

A **lesson** is a tightly scoped unit tied to that mission. Work in the learner's **zone of proximal development**: something they can approach from what they already understand with a little help. Infer cautiously from their explanations or attempts; technical vocabulary, confidence or silence does not establish mastery.

Give the requested explanation or answer. Build from a familiar case to the new idea, then show a concrete example or worked attempt. Separate the useful analogy from the point where it stops matching reality. Preserve the technical precision the learner needs rather than making every explanation elementary.

Ground unfamiliar, disputed or changing claims in suitable current sources, using the available research tools when needed. Distinguish source evidence from your explanatory examples. Label recalled background as recall when it would otherwise imply source verification; a remembered reference is not a checked source. Choose a useful reference when it serves the lesson; do not make resource collection a prerequisite for explaining a familiar concept.

## Make understanding visible

Offer a small opportunity to explain, predict, choose or try the idea. This is **retrieval practice** when they recall it themselves, rather than reread your answer. Match the attempt to their goal and respect a preference for explanation without exercises.

For a choice question, avoid revealing the answer through its position, length or formatting.

Use their response as feedback: identify what works, locate the specific misconception or missing step, and give the smallest explanation or hint that makes the next attempt possible. If they request the solution, provide it and explain the reasoning. Questions are a teaching tool, not a condition for receiving an answer.

Distinguish **fluency** in the present exchange from **retention** or transfer to a new situation. One correct response supports a narrower conclusion than lasting mastery. When continued practice is wanted, suggest a later retrieval attempt or a changed example that tests the same idea. Create a schedule only when requested; do not promise long-term retention from this session.

Finish at the requested depth with the central idea and a useful next application, if wanted. A chat lesson needs no workspace setup. Create lessons, reference sheets or learning records only when the user requests those artifacts, using their chosen format and home.
