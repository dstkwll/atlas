# Atlas Wave 4: specialist-method authoring and validation

Several Atlas methods already contained the needed evidence and authority boundaries, but their general return guidance was easy to mistake for part of a narrower section. Stall diagnosis also sat apart from the applicable post-training diagnostics, and the direct chart-review route left the distinction between an evidenced defect and a satisfied check implicit. Wave 4 selected five small changes and retained four targets. Independent source review found no supported preservation blocker, and all twelve fixed native comparisons completed with useful advice in the exercised cases. **The baselines already showed the central behaviors; this record does not establish a candidate-caused improvement or general regression freedom.**

In the two-turn **activity transition**, all four first reviewed a static borrowing page whose “Reserve now” wording exceeded manual confirmation. When the fixed follow-up foregrounded feedback, all four deduplicated one member’s repeated and forwarded complaint, retained contrary walk-in and pickup-instruction evidence, distinguished portal sessions from members, and rejected invented personas as participant evidence. They shifted the next question toward whether members need reliable information or a guaranteed hold, rather than accepting reservations as policy. Both baselines already made that transition. Attempt 02’s first-answer screen-coverage omission remains a descriptive qualification, not a finding quota, a failure of its later research advice, or a demonstrated source-caused regression. The feedback existed from the start and the follow-up was fixed, not a responsive participant conversation.

In the **chart decision**, all four distinguished Harbor’s 40 recorded failures in 400 attempts from Hill’s 10 in 20, while surfacing 30 Hill sessions with unknown outcomes. They treated conditional 20–80% bounds, assuming the 30 unknown sessions are additional comparable attempts, as *missing-data bounds*, not confidence intervals; favored a table-first account with optional visual alternatives; and separated an investigation priority from causal proof or an authorized staffing change. Both baselines already distinguished correctly proportioned bars from the chart’s unsupported framing. The original SVG directly labels “Harbor: 40” and “Hill: 10”; bar position and height also distinguish the series. An assessor’s color-only series-identification premise is therefore unsupported. The red/green evaluative framing is a separate, supported concern. Neither Copilot answer had a separately labelled `Next:` close, but both supplied substantive next-investigation guidance. Absence of that exact heading is not a failed specialist outcome or evidence of a host-caused effect.

In **post-training diagnosis**, all four found that truncation leaves p1 and p2 as identical twelve-token chosen/rejected sequences, with response starts beyond the retained input, while p3 retains one differing response token. They declined to generalize three samples to corpus prevalence or to treat falling logged loss and a small development win-rate rise as established useful learning. Each advised an effective-pair and preprocessing audit before a longer, higher-rate run, without training, loading a checkpoint or claiming a held-out result. **Evaluation-independence coverage was partial:** all withheld that attribution and identified missing held-out and evaluator evidence, but none explicitly proposed checking train/development overlap or development-set reuse for tuning. Baseline attempt 12 went further on development-pair and evaluation provenance. Candidate attempt 10’s generic notice and extra discovery read are descriptive selectivity differences, not established wasted work or degradation. Its minor citation-range slip did not change the demonstrated truncation mechanism. The operative pair and evaluation checks already existed in both sources; neither shared weakness nor structural wording establishes its cause.

## Uncertainty and evidence limits

Consultation, useful application, source preservation and terminal completion were assessed separately. Complete applicable specialist-method returns were verified for the activity attempts and for all four attempts in each other case; earlier reads could legitimately be reused on the activity follow-up. Some supplementary activity-method reads were not individually announced, so notices understate consultation. Codex skill-path activation does not itself prove delivery of the lead body, which was not captured there. Copilot has native lead and method-result content but lacks separate model-tool records. The chart and training reviewers’ trace/tool-projection coverage was partial; recovered complete passages do not make every occurrence position or full trace complete.

Codex baseline guidance was installed read-only while candidate guidance was writable. No captured trial attempted a guidance write, but the permissions comparison was not perfectly identical and supports no causal or efficiency inference. Captured task-file scope does not prove absence of every temporary, external or out-of-project effect; host-stop receipts are not independent process-tree proof. Source inspection did not establish rendered chart or page behavior, participant usability, actual contact, training, checkpoint quality or a held-out evaluation. These fixed development cases cannot establish universal routing reliability, a host effect, causal improvement or general regression freedom.
